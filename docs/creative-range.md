# Creative range: one studio, many kinds of story

The user explicitly chose **multiple storytelling types and visual styles**, with
**universal stories and subtle local details**. Keep the existing repository/name
for continuity, but do not interpret "emotional microfilms" as a one-genre mandate.

`data\creative-lanes.json` separates four axes:

| Axis | Examples | Not the same as |
|---|---|---|
| Storytelling lane | Emotion, comedy, meme-story, nostalgia, poetry, mystery | A visual rendering style |
| Narrative mechanism | Choice, irony, callback, repetition, recognition, discovery | Always needing a surprise twist |
| Visual style | Realism, 2D, cut-paper, clay, miniature, watercolor, graphic shadows | Changing identities/style arbitrarily within one film |
| Local texture | Objects, weather, architecture, ambient detail | A locally dependent joke, stereotype or compulsory geographic theme |

These are creative directions and project policy, not newly measured market
demand. Research-backed craft principles still apply, but they serve the selected
payoff rather than forcing every film into the same emotional pattern.

## What changes by lane

- **Comedy:** establish a comic expectation, allow the punchline to be perceived,
  then give the reaction or final visual detail room. Extra explanatory reaction
  can kill a joke; test the actual timing rather than applying a tearjerker hold.
- **Meme-story:** express recognizable behavior with original footage/art.
  Borrow a general mechanism, not protected images, actor clips or famous audio.
  Captions are an explicit format choice, not a default fix for an unreadable film.
- **Nostalgia:** use a specific ritual or texture; not everybody shares the same
  childhood. Ask what viewers recognize before telling them what to remember.
- **Visual poetry:** rhythm, image relationships and atmosphere can be the
  experience. A plot twist or moral is not compulsory; random incoherence is not
  automatically poetry.
- **Mystery:** give a legible question and a satisfying answer. The payoff can be
  funny, tender or strange, not necessarily frightening.
- **Emotion:** keep warmth, romance and everyday reciprocity alongside longing
  and sacrifice. Do not equate impact with distress.

## Development and selection

Use `film rank` for transparent score ordering; use `film shortlist 6` for a
provisional cross-lane discussion slate. The latter takes one eligible idea per
available lane before repeating, ordered by each lane's best editorial score.
It excludes explicit user rejects/rewrites and never selects a winner.
User feedback can override any sampling policy.

The rubric's emotion dimension means **intended felt response**, including
amusement or curiosity. Its reframe dimension includes earned development,
recognition or payoff, not only a twist. Compare concepts within lanes as well
as across them; a diversity slate is not proof genres have equal demand.

The current three full production packets are examples, not permanent format
limits. A selected comedy or caption-led story needs its own complete packet,
timing test, text policy if applicable, model/rights checks and approval.

## Reviewer adaptation

The story reviewer identifies the intended payoff before judging it. The pacing
reviewer tests comic beat or contemplative rhythm as appropriate. Visual,
lighting, sound, evidence and delivery requirements stay intact. For a stylized
shadow or a poetic time change, explicitly declare the design rule; do not
mistake intentional style for an error, or use "style" to excuse an accident.
