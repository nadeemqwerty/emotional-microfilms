## Organic action and sound are acceptance requirements

### A film needs causal progression, not just animated scene illustrations

For a discovery/memory story, specify the activity before discovery, physical
object reveal and handling, the recognition, a motivated entry into one specific
live memory, an actual response there, and a changed present-day expression.
Do not begin with the person already posed beside the final story prop and
assume a later cut supplies the missing discovery.

Every transition needs actual outgoing/incoming composition, eyeline/action,
prop and light continuity, camera/focus behavior and sound intent. A portrait
push needs a genuinely moving shot and matching face geometry at the memory
entry; a slow digital enlargement of an unrelated still is not that proof.
Age/time-plane changes occur at designed edits, not through an accidental face
morph. Inspect the motion around each actual boundary, not endpoints alone.

Keep the 8-15s/three-shot default. When an explicitly requested narrative needs
more room, a scenario may opt into `formatOverride` with a written reason,
`maximumSeconds` no greater than 30 and `maximumShots` no greater than five.
This changes that story's validation/assembly limits only; it grants no spending,
reference, playback or release approval. A larger number is not itself better pacing.

For quality experiments, keep the same cast, endpoint images, duration and
requested action when comparing models. A different memory action is a separate
scenario test, not a clean model comparison. Log exact quotes, configurations,
source IDs/hashes and actual timecoded findings; retain failures and choose on
observed narrative, acting, physics, camera, cut and sound evidence—not model
price, resolution labels or "state of the art" marketing.

A technically valid ellipse is not automatically a good emotional transition.
The user rejected the silent first pilot and its skipped sitting action. When
physical acceptance matters, show the turn, weight shift, lowering, seat contact
and settle; inspect actual motion and both boundary poses. Do not mask a missing
action with a dissolve that ghosts bodies, or call a teleport organic.

Muted story proof is an intermediate test, not permission to deliver an
unrequested silent finish. Plan a continuous ambience/music route and rights
before motion, then verify the real track in the exported file. The retained
earlier father/toddler preview used original local keyboard synthesis and
filtered-noise rain, not an invented OpenArt music endpoint or a copied song.
Its music was subsequently rejected, not established as a studio default.
Meters/stream checks establish
presence, continuity and level only. Until actual listening and joined playback
occur, label it a review preview and leave sound, entertainment and human final
approval pending. User rejection supersedes an earlier agent creative pass;
preserve the rejected take and diagnose the actual complaint.

Compare native sound **between scenes**, not just against clipping. Actual attic
and portrait-entry sources differed by roughly 40 dB in mean level despite both
having audio streams. A whole-film gain cannot repair that relative mismatch.
Preserve good picture and originals: a measured per-source audio-only derivative
can copy encoded video packets unchanged. Retain source history/hash, gain,
transform, matching picture-packet hashes, derivative hash and fresh full decode,
frame count and level evidence. Transfer bounded visual inspection only through
that verified identity; do not manufacture a new playback/listening pass.
Use scene intent rather than forcing quiet room tone and child action to the
same level. Gain does not remove a heavy arrangement, verify native sound content
or implement a J-cut/crossfade. Recheck actual export boundaries and require joined
listening before any sound approval.

## Required sequence and review stops

| Stage | Actual artifact and decision | What remains blocked |
|---|---|---|
| `preproduction` | Complete provisional frame-wise requirements, character beauty/performance designs and bounded authorization for the first real cast image | Scene and video generation |
| `characters-approved` | Real original cast images; user accepts faces, ages, wardrobe, style and relative scale | Motion; scene images without those references |
| `keyframes-approved` | All literal scene endpoints and every changed composition/light state; side-by-side user review | Motion until the full design is locked |
| `design-locked` | User accepts script, opening hook, beat timing, scene transitions, camera, lights/shadows, effects and sound route together | Unapproved alterations and unquoted jobs |
| `motion-approved` | Actual clips pass motion, identities, effects, contact, light continuity and achieved timing | Final export until sound and final reviews pass |

Show the first real cast image before buying scene images. Generate a scene
candidate, show it and incorporate feedback before paying for dependent variants.
Do not silently turn an iterative request into a large batch. A user may explicitly
approve a bounded batch, but character and final scene review stops still apply.
Each new paid step needs the applicable quote, authorization and remaining budget.

Before that first generation, complete `framePlan` for the whole intended film:
declared delivery FPS, contiguous integer ranges, actual shot/beat association,
story/attention, every identity's visible/offscreen performance, camera,
light/shadow change, initial/intermediate/final motion and contact physics,
animation style, canonical effects/audio, transitions and acceptance.
The [reviewed frame contract](completion-plan.md#9-pre-generation-framebeat-requirements-and-beautiful-characters)
and four `examples` packets are the schema/working references. This is a
provisional specification, reconciled against actual accepted cast/scenes before
motion, not an assertion that nonexistent media was inspected.

Beautiful characters means original appealing/charming faces or silhouettes,
distinctive features, natural detail, coherent wardrobe/grooming and an
age-appropriate chosen style. State observable acceptance and what to avoid.
Retain diversity; do not mandate a skin tone, celebrity likeness, homogenized
beauty filter or adult presentation for children. An existing accepted cast
does not need replacement merely because the new design field exists.

Use `characterPlan.references[].prompt` for the actual character image, not the
older scene-oriented `identityBible.anchorPrompt` study. A neutral cast reference
is NOT the film's first frame. Pair references can save credits only if both faces,
wardrobe and scale are readable; otherwise propose separate references and quote
the additional work. Changing a story location need not mean regenerating good cast.
The actual first cast take includes a visible studio softbox and a facial-key
direction that does not fully match the prompt. Those are reference-background
caveats, not approved scene design. Preserve accepted faces, hair, wardrobe and
scale only; explicitly exclude studio equipment and relight the actual location.
User character acceptance remains independent of this operator assessment.

For multi-age families, isolate each approved identity before scene work when a
group sheet or dominant photograph could substitute the wrong person. Exact
source crops can preserve good faces without another paid casting run: retain
source hash, crop transform, derivative hash and accepted upload reference.
Actually inspect the crops; reference-background fragments are not scene blocking.
Declare separate `characterPlan.references` with identity `covers`, then select
the relevant reference IDs in each keyframe's `characterReferenceIds`. Omission
retains the legacy all-cast requirement; explicit selections cannot be empty,
duplicate, unknown or satisfied by a different cast member. All identities still
need actual cast approval. A parent printed in a photograph is a prop reference,
not permission to replace the live teen with that parent. Inspect age, face,
wardrobe and time plane before buying any dependent motion; retain failed takes.
Declare `sceneReferenceIds` when both a geometry reference and exact prop content
are required; preflight requires every declared genuine frame. Inspect physical
viewpoint too: a person studying a photograph may show its back to the camera.
Never accept a portrait front with an easel stand drawn over it. An over-shoulder
reverse angle can reveal the front naturally; plan and inspect both sides.
For an intended exact adjacent boundary, reuse the genuine image under both
scene roles instead of buying a subtly different approximation. Record the
alias, original job/source, byte-identical hashes and zero new-generation cost.
Reconcile actual face position, gaze and crop in the design. This eliminates
still-reference drift, not unverified video endpoint drift: inspect the actual
last/first frames and the ensuing live performance before accepting the cut.

Conform to observed poses, not provisional screen coordinates. If a good child
reaction has its eyes near 22% rather than a planned 42%, guide the ungenerated
return with that actual composition instead of rerolling the child for a number.
Preserve the submitted request as history and separately record the conformed
design. A pointing child looking up-right cannot cut to an already completed
leftward response without an intentional ellipse: show the arm lowering and
attention returning, or design another observable transition.

An action variant must be cross-read through **every department**, not just its
motion prompt. Trace identity/props, continuity, pacing, direction beats,
per-actor intent/gaze, interval initial/intermediate/final states and contacts,
camera segments/framing, shadow casters/receivers, sound cues and both cut reasons.
A standing bubble-play candidate must not retain positive swing instructions in
its shadow map or camera motivation. A source-motivated physical push-in must be
declared consistently, not simultaneously called locked or faked with a crop.
Reconcile all affected packets before design lock; keyword checks and filled
fields cannot replace this semantic review or prove the generated result.

The first scene uses accepted character references. Later scene images also use
accepted geometry/prop references where useful. Preserve faces/wardrobe, but
deliberately relight and restage for the actual scene. Do not copy neutral reference
lighting into every shot. Account for all changed shot sizes, worlds, seasons,
poses and light states. If an important intermediate state needs an anchor, create
it and split the shot or use a tested supported route; do not invent a middle-frame
API field on a model that only accepts start/end.
The current edit path supports cuts, ellipses and graphic match cuts, not an
automatic dissolve/transition renderer. Other transitions need a tested extension
or editor workflow before claiming support.

## An opening hook is a designed shot, not a sparkle preset

Every packet requires `hookPlan`: the actual first-frame evidence, first motion,
inspection window, honest question, promised payoff and test. Inspect the literal
0s frame and a short opening excerpt muted at phone size. Identify the focal point,
what can be understood before explanation, and whether UI/crop/effects hide it.
One second is an initial project inspection choice, not a universal hook law.

Possible mechanisms: an intriguing relationship, visual contradiction, unexpected
scale, a beautiful revealed detail, incomplete purposeful action, or a strong
silhouette/color contrast. A golden butterfly can direct attention, but a swarm
covering the child's face can destroy the actual hook. Motion for its own sake,
flashing highlights and misleading shock are not evidence of reduced swiping.
Compare proposed compositions first; buy additional variants only with approval.
Audience retention remains unmeasured until an actual declared experiment runs.

## Atmosphere and beauty palette

Consider this menu at style development, not as a ban on expressive imagery.
Choose a coherent world/season and a small motion budget, not every effect at once.

| Motif | Potential story/attention job | Design and failure checks |
|---|---|---|
| Glitter / sparkle dust | A controlled reveal accent or material sparkle | Declare reflective/emissive material, grain size, count/density, intensity and no-strobe behavior; keep eyes/hands legible |
| Neon glow / trails | Stylized energy, color rhythm or a purposeful path | Distinguish graphic halo from physical emission; stable tracking, controlled spill, no trails through bodies or clipped skin |
| Golden shiny butterflies | A gentle gaze guide, wonder or a response accent | Count/path, wing form, foreground/background depth, no face occlusion; distinguish reflected gold from true emission |
| Golden butterfly dust | A fine sparkle wake, magical detail or gaze guide | Dust has its own timed path/lifetime, depth and scale; no random swarms, mandatory glow or concealed action |
| Flowers / a bloom | Reveal detail, renewal, an offered gift | Species/scale and stable petal geometry; growth is a separate primary action, not an assumed free background effect |
| Sakura / drifting petals | Soft directional rhythm and environmental depth | Coherent season, wind and scale; petals do not become fingers, snow or facial features |
| Sakura / flower dust | A softer floral particle veil, distinct from full petals | Specify pink/material/scale and density; avoid snow/confetti noise and unmotivated emission |
| Rain | Texture, vulnerability, shelter or reflection | Wetness, rate, gravity/wind, shelter boundary and readable dry evidence |
| Trees moving / breeze | Living background without another plot event | Subtle leaf motion, rooted trunks, consistent wind; no warped faces/architecture |
| Autumn leaves | Warm texture, change or a motion guide | Species/season and fall direction; avoid unexplained simultaneous spring blossoms |
| Illuminated props / fireflies | An attention anchor or motivated light-state change | Source position, plausible spill/reflection, contact/cast shadows, exposure and stable brightness |
| Highlights / restrained bloom | Separate a subject or emphasize a valued detail | Preserve skin/eye detail and the reveal; no strobe, clipped halos or arbitrary glowing outlines |

These are creative hypotheses, not guaranteed happiness, dopamine or reach.
Select one main performance action and a restrained secondary environmental layer.
Visual poetry may make the environment itself the main action. Do not prohibit
beauty merely because a grounded baseline is cheaper; show alternatives and cost.

The shared machine-readable menu is [visual-effects.json](../data/visual-effects.json).
Inspect it with `npm run film -- effects`. Its 14 named creative treatments are
planning options, not model parameters, installed renderers or quality passes.
Select a coherent subset or explicitly none with an omission rationale.
Custom treatments may be planned with the same cue controls and real route
preflight; a preset label never replaces a complete canonical effect cue.

## Required effect cue sheet

`atmospherePlan` records the motion budget, chosen effects and why alternatives
are omitted. Each effect has an ID, active shots, from/to times, purpose, depth,
motion, occlusion, light interaction, shadow interaction, continuity and fallback.
New frame-planned productions also require `appearance` (material/color),
`density`, `intensity`, `scale` and `lightMode` (reflection/emission/graphic glow).
Frame intervals name the exact active effect IDs; the resolved script retains
their clipped subevent timing alongside every overlapping sound cue.

Illustrative planning cue, not permission to decorate an existing film or an
invented provider parameter:

```json
{
  "id": "gold-dust",
  "route": "in-generation",
  "shotIds": ["a"],
  "from": 0,
  "to": 5,
  "referenceFrameIds": ["a-start", "a-end"],
  "purpose": "Guide the gaze to one revealed detail.",
  "appearance": "Fine warm golden reflective motes; distinct from full butterfly wings.",
  "density": "Sparse, approximately eight visible grains; clear central faces and hands.",
  "intensity": "Subtle highlight sparkle below the face exposure; no flashing.",
  "scale": "Tiny grains at background depth, not oversized confetti.",
  "lightMode": "Reflective gold, not an emitting source; no new skin spill.",
  "depth": "Behind the principals, before the distant background.",
  "motion": "One gentle coherent drift, continuous entry and exit.",
  "occlusion": "Pass behind bodies and props; never through them.",
  "lightInteraction": "Reflect the declared warm source without changing the key.",
  "shadowInteraction": "Negligible grain shadows; retain grounded subject shadows.",
  "continuity": "Stable color, scale, path and brightness across the range.",
  "fallback": "Reduce to a static reflected accent or omit if motion fails."
}
```

For emissive neon or golden dust, replace the reflective assumption with an
explicit source intensity/spill decision, affected receivers and coherent
shadows/exposure. Tiny distant emission may have negligible spill; bright nearby
emission cannot be justified by adding only a halo. Model/editor controls and
actual result quality must be checked separately.
`in-generation` effects must be designed in every affected literal endpoint and
the actual motion prompt. Endpoints establish intended appearance, not successful
motion. Inspect flutter, drift, deformation and light pumping in actual playback.

`composite` effects require an actual registered `effect` asset, rights evidence
and an `effect-preflight` review before design lock. Test alpha/edges, perspective,
masking around bodies/props, tracking, spill and the phone-size result. The current
FFmpeg assembler does NOT generate butterflies, masks, tracking or relighting.
Use a capable editor for the actual composite, register the resulting clip/hash
and review it. A route label is not an implemented compositor.

Changing the effect, its timing or light state changes the production packet and
invalidates the affected design/motion approvals. Reuse unchanged media where
possible; no automatic full-film rerender.

## Light and shadow integration

Read [lighting/shadows](lighting-shadows.md) for key/fill, exposure and caster/
receiver mapping. Emissive objects, reflected highlights and lens/post glow are
different. Glow added around a butterfly does not by itself relight a nearby face.
Decide whether spill is negligible, intentionally added, or requires another
approved lighting state. Map affected skin, wood, cloth and water highlights;
keep shadows consistent with the declared source and depth. Tiny distant lights
should not create unexplained large opposing shadows. Fantasy exceptions must
be explicit and coherent rather than accidental artifacts.

Official technical sources inspected: [Blender Emission](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/render/shader_nodes/shader/emission.html)
describes emitting surfaces/light color and strength; [Glare](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/compositing/types/filter/glare.html)
describes bright-region bloom/glow and threshold/clamp controls. These establish
rendering concepts, not OpenArt controls or psychological effects. No Blender
installation, effect rendering or generated-output benchmark is claimed.

## Provenance and executable checks

For `character` and `keyframe` assets, record `assetSources[relativePath]` before
CLI registration: `origin:"generated"` and actual `sourceHistoryId`, or
`origin:"authorized-reference"` plus real permission evidence. A generated asset's
SHA-256 must occur in that completed job's `outputAssetHashes`, recorded from the
actual downloaded output. Preserve genuine provider objects in `characterReferences`
and `keyframeReferences`. A sketch/missing provenance cannot pass the image gate.
Local checks cannot authenticate a remote image or human testimony: never forge
job metadata, permissions, inspection or user approval to satisfy them.

Record `characters` and `characters-user` reviews, then advance to
`characters-approved`. Character reviews bind just the cast identity/assets/refs;
adding a scene image does not invalidate an unchanged cast approval. Replacing
cast or changing faces/wardrobe does. Use `validateSceneReferences` before paid
guided scene generation.

Record `keyframes` and `keyframes-user`, advance to `keyframes-approved`, then
record actual `design-user` evidence and advance to `design-locked`. This is the
final packet lock, not permission to spend. Set `nextGenerationKind` to
`character`, `scene` or `motion` for the respective stage; call `validateSpend`
and, for video, `validateMotionReferences` against the fully resolved request.
Source/design reviews ignore subsequently added video assets, so submitting a
second approved shot does not require pretending the source images changed.

No automatic paid client exists. Old manifests need explicit refresh, the new
packet fields and genuine missing reviews; do not silently promote legacy
`keyframes-approved` state into a locked design.

A failed take clears generation approval and blocks advancement. Diagnose it and
obtain/reassert only the specifically applicable repair authorization, fresh
request/quote and remaining budget before another call. The earlier-stage repair
must remain possible without falsely passing the failed image; replace the actual
asset and repeat its affected reviews. No automatic retries or approval recycling.

## Route capability and demonstration ledger

This is not a compositor feature list. Shared in-generation workflow: put the
motif in literal endpoints AND exact motion prompts, quote the real supported
model/form, generate only with consent, download/hash and inspect the whole clip.
Shared composite workflow: use an available capable editor with original/cleared
assets, document layers/masks/tracking/light treatment, export/register the
resulting clip and pass `effect-preflight`. No such editor is installed or
exercised by this implementation; composite execution remains BLOCKED until it is.

|---|---|---|---|
| Golden butterflies | In-generation for simple stable count/path; tracked/masked editor composite for controlled foreground flight; cue a gaze/payoff, never hide it | Gold reflection is not emission; inspect wing stability, face occlusion, spill/shadows. Fallback: one nonemissive distant butterfly or omit | NOT DEMONSTRATED |
| Glitter / golden butterfly dust | Designed endpoints/motion or cleared particle layers with depth/occlusion; use explicit density, scale and timed entry/exit | Reflective versus emissive; no strobe, unmotivated spill or face occlusion. | Neon glow / trails | Generated accepted light states or a capable tracked/relit editor; distinguish graphic post glow from physical emission | Inspect tracked path, body occlusion, skin detail and actual light interaction. route/model/editor settings, inspection scope and reviewer findings privately.
The tested assembler performs trim/fit/cuts/audio, not overlays, tracking, masks
or relighting. Do not mark one motif operational from another's demonstration.
Select effects for beauty/enjoyment under the entertainment analyst, not because
the menu exists. No extra generation or pilot rerender is authorized by this table.

Use the typed personal/delegated authority contract in
[workflow](workflow.md); earlier `*-user` examples apply only to genuine user
viewing. Preserve active producer decisions rather than migrating their labels
again. A rejected cut remains rejected until diagnosed repair/review.
