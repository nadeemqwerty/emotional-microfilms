# Emotional music, attention, hooks and brain-reward evidence

Research accessed 30 October 2026, IST. **Operational objective:** an intelligible
expectation, a meaningful emotional outcome and time to recognize it. We can test
those experiences; we cannot infer or maximize dopamine from a Reel's metrics.

This is a production playbook, not a medical intervention or a neurochemical
measurement protocol. Its rules are implemented in the music/pacing skill,
pacing/evidence/sound reviewers, packet schema and final review gate.

## What the evidence actually measures

PET radioligand findings, fMRI BOLD activity, self-reported pleasure/chills,
physiological arousal and watch time are different endpoints. A reward-related
region becoming active does not measure a neurotransmitter. Chills and looping
do not establish a dopamine quantity or a maximum.

**FT** = relevant full methods/results/discussion inspected. **A** = legitimate
abstract inspected. **AR** = authoritative abstract record. Sample sizes are
included only when verified in the material read.

| ID / source | Design and endpoint | Supported finding and limit |
|---|---|---|
| N1: [Salimpoor et al. 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21217764/), doi:10.1038/nn.2726, A | Raclopride PET, autonomic measures, separate fMRI with same listeners/stimuli | Pleasurable music associated with striatal dopamine release. Anticipatory caudate / peak nucleus-accumbens timing investigated with fMRI; PET is not a second-by-second trace. N not stated in abstract read. |
| N2: [Ferreri et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6397525/), doi:10.1073/pnas.1811878116, FT | Randomized-sequence double-blind within-person crossover, dopaminergic drug conditions/placebo; 27 analyzed; 45s excerpts; pleasure, chills, electrodermal activity and willingness to pay | Causal evidence for dopaminergic involvement in musical reward, not direct release measurement or dopamine as sole cause. Post-song pleasantness contrast marginal (p=.052); only 16/27 had placebo chills; exclusions constrain transfer. |
| N3: [Gold et al. 2019: musical reward prediction errors](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6386687/), doi:10.1073/pnas.1809855116, FT | 20 final participants; visual choices probabilistically selected consonant/dissonant endings; 25.6s Bach chorales, 60 trials; learning model and fMRI | Music reinforced choices and nucleus-accumbens BOLD tracked modeled reward-prediction errors in this task. Not dopamine measurement; learning subsets smaller. See N7 critique. |
| N4: [Gold et al. 2019: predictability and uncertainty](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6867811/), doi:10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0428-19.2019, FT | Behavioral studies, 43 and 27 analyzed; approximately 30s monophonic excerpts; modeled complexity and liking; repeated exposure in study 2 | Intermediate complexity related to liking; predictable outcomes preferred in uncertain contexts. Repetition reduced liking within task. Western-tonal model/preferences/exclusions constrain generalization; no dopamine endpoint. |
| N5: [Cheung et al. 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31708393/), doi:10.1016/j.cub.2019.09.067, A | Model of 80,000 Western-pop chords plus behavioral ratings/fMRI; chord count is not participant N | Uncertainty interacted with surprise in pleasure. Nucleus accumbens reflected uncertainty, not the pleasure interaction reported elsewhere. No universal "surprising chord = reward spike". |
| N6: [Berridge, Robinson & Aldridge 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2756052/), doi:10.1016/j.coph.2008.12.014, FT review | Behavioral/animal/pharmacological/neuroscience synthesis | Distinguish hedonic liking, incentive wanting and learning. They can dissociate. Do not overcorrect into "dopamine never influences human musical pleasure"; N2 matters. |
| N7: [de Fleurian et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6800363/), doi:10.1073/pnas.1913244116, FT critique/reanalysis | Methodological response to N3 | Sensory surprise is not identical to reward-prediction error; visual cues and influential observations limit extrapolation. Not an independent experiment disproving all music reinforcement. |
| N8: [Gruber, Gelman & Ranganath 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4252494/), doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2014.08.060, FT | Trivia curiosity, fMRI, incidental faces and memory; anticipation alone 14s | Curiosity related to reward/memory-region activity and later memory. Authors caution fMRI cannot establish increased dopamine release. No first-second social hook test; N in uninspected supplements omitted. |
| N9: [Kang et al. 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19619181/), doi:10.1111/j.1467-9280.2009.02402.x, A | Trivia, fMRI, separate behavioral information-seeking and recall studies | Curiosity related to seeking answers and memory. Correlational BOLD is not dopamine; no measured Reels sharing or cinematic delay optimum. |
| N10: [Magliano & Zacks 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3208769/), doi:10.1111/j.1551-6709.2011.01202.x, FT | Reanalysis of 33-minute narrative film; behavioral groups 41/24, fMRI from latter study | Meaningful action discontinuities mattered more for event segmentation than camera changes alone. Naturalistic associations, not a randomized pacing recipe. Expands the earlier abstract-only E3 read. |
| N11: [Lang et al. 2000](https://scholars.cityu.edu.hk/en/publications/the-effects-of-edits-on-arousal-attention-and-memory-for-televisi/), doi:10.1207/s15506878jobem4401_7, AR | Manipulated within-scene camera-change rate; arousal/memory measures | Abstract reports increased arousal and memory within tested conditions. Neither "fast edits always harm memory" nor "all fast scene changes help" follows. N/rate thresholds unavailable. |

Attention capture is not comprehension. Full methods/N not read. |

Confidence is relatively strong for the need to distinguish endpoints; moderate
and task-specific for musical expectancy/curiosity/event segmentation. **Confidence
in any exact ten-second cue recipe remains low until tested in this project.**

## Translate findings into decisions

| Distinction | Production rule | Reviewer question |
|---|---|---|
| Wanting / liking / learning [N2,N6] | Optimize meaningful experience, not a single supposed brain chemical | Are we measuring what the claim says? |
| Surprise / valued outcome [N3-N5,N7] | Establish a pattern or social expectation before changing it | Does the reveal reward understanding or merely confuse? |
| Curiosity / missing information [N8,N9] | Offer an open-but-answerable visual question | Can a first viewer state the question? |
| Orienting / comprehension [N10-N12] | Related cuts protect action; arbitrary changes require justification | What causal evidence is lost at the cut? |
| Tempo / pacing | Independently plan beat, performance, information and cut rhythms | Is "faster" hiding the choice? |
| Valence / arousal / pleasure [N2] | Ask about warmth, tension, intensity and liking separately | Did stronger intensity actually improve the intended feeling? |
| Laboratory / microfilm | Test the short-form adaptation instead of compressing published timings | What project evidence supports this precise timing? |

### Hooks to test, not biological tricks

- **Legible unusual action:** why is someone protecting an empty seat?
- **Social choice:** who will receive the larger share or the last pool of light?
- **Resolvable question:** will a familiar gesture receive an answer?

The ending must repay the opening. Do not equate inaccessible clues with mystery,
or confuse shock, louder sound or ever-more frequent changes with reward.

## Dedicated pacing rules

Use [pacing.md](pacing.md) and each packet's validated beat timeline.
**Setup -> anticipation -> readable turn -> chosen response -> emotional hold**
is our working architecture, not a neurological law.

N10-N12 support distinguishing camera transitions from meaningful event changes.
They do not supply a universal harmful/helpful cuts-per-second threshold.
N2-N4 used excerpts around 25.6-45 seconds; N8 anticipation alone lasted 14
seconds. Their times cannot be compressed proportionally into a ten-second film.

Selective music/action alignment is worth testing. So is an unchanged gentle cue
over a hesitant action. Neither synchronization nor counterpoint is established
as superior by this source set. Keep a sound bridge if it helps the physical
relationship survive a cut; allow a brief music gap without eliminating room tone.

## Three concrete cue recipes

All instrumentation, harmony, pulse and timestamps below are **creative
hypotheses**. Use original/licensed material and align to actual generated motion,
not the requested motion. A music service may not produce exact beat timing;
an editor or composer must conform it.

### 1. The Dry Seat: small relief

| Time | Picture/attention | Candidate sound |
|---|---|---|
| 0-1.5s | Coat seems to reserve seat; wet stranger | Sheltered rain and one sparse soft note; no instant emotional declaration |
| 1.5-3.5s | Coat begins moving | Tactile cloth foley; two-note motif with space, not accelerating percussion |
| 3.5-5s | Dry patch and invitation | Brief reduction of musical density so evidence reads |
| 5-7.5s | Ellipsis to accepted seat and gaze | Continuous rain bridge; warm harmonic opening after recognition |
| 7.5-10s | Shared relieved smile | Let resonance and room tone decay; no new hook |

Try approximately 96 BPM if a pulse is useful: 0.625s per beat, 16 beats/10s.
That is a synchronization convenience, not a preferred dopamine frequency. A
floating cue with no obvious pulse is a valid alternative.

### 2. Same Old Handshake: recognition

| Time | Picture/attention | Candidate sound |
|---|---|---|
| 0-1.5s | Uncertain adult, apparently dormant robot | Quiet workshop room tone, narrow soft sustained interval |
| 1.5-3.8s | Robot's two visible taps | Precisely matched small knocks, not an unexplained electronic melody |
| 3.8-5s | Recognition begins | Thin the score rather than announcing a huge reveal |
| 5-8s | Human returns taps and places hand nearby | Reply taps, modest timbral warmth; align resolution with response, not necessarily cut |
| 8-10s | Quiet proximity | Hold and decay; keep eyes steady, no power-down sound |

If native generation reliably supplies useful contact sound, compare actual
accepted outputs and edit time. Do not assume it gives isolated stems.

### 3. The Last Light: gentle sacrifice

| Time | Picture/attention | Candidate sound |
|---|---|---|
| 0-2s | Warm robot, seedling in shadow | Low greenhouse wind and a sparse open interval |
| 2-4.5s | Robot notices and reaches | Small mechanical texture, no ominous escalating pulse |
| 4.5-5.5s | Cut to changed light state | One justified swivel click bridging cut; music may pause |
| 5.5-7.5s | Robot accepts shadow, plant receives light | Small consonant opening; avoid death-coded descent |
| 7.5-10s | Cost and benefit held together | Soft unresolved or settled decay; test both |

If viewers infer robot death, revise picture and sound together only after
identifying the error; don't claim it was a successful "bigger emotion".

## Experiment recipes

**Audio:** lock picture, duration, captions and foley. Randomly assign fresh
first-time viewers to foley-led, selectively aligned music or understated
counterpoint. Control playback conditions; compare levels sensibly, not by making
silence "equally loud". Record music familiarity/preference where relevant.

**Pacing:** same story/footage/outcome; initially equal duration and shot count.
Move one boundary: earlier reveal/longer response, more anticipation, or clearer
setup. Keep audio identical for picture-only testing. If audio is retimed, label
it audiovisual pacing, not a pure cut-point effect.

**Native versus separate:** different images and sound mean a whole-production
comparison. Score usable sync, first-view meaning, unwanted voices/music, rights,
repair time and cost per accepted film. It cannot isolate a music effect.

Predeclare essential comprehension as the initial quality gate. Choose a main
outcome before testing: first-view understanding or meaningful felt emotion.
Secondary ratings can separate pleasure, warmth/valence, arousal, tension,
authenticity and irritation. Sharing intention is not actual sharing; where
feasible record voluntary behavior without pressure. Platform metrics remain
exploratory until distribution differences are understood.

Ask why a viewer replays: enjoyment, discovering a detail, or not understanding.
Do not declare a winner from a tiny fluctuation or a small convenience panel.
No drugs, physiological manipulation or neurochemical inference is needed.

## Claims the evidence reviewer must reject

| Unsupported | Defensible wording |
|---|---|
| "This soundtrack maximizes dopamine." | "This cue is hypothesized to improve the intended emotional experience." |
| "Chills prove a dopamine spike." | "Chills are one reported response; they do not quantify dopamine." |
| "Reward-center BOLD measures dopamine." | "BOLD is an indirect hemodynamic measure." |
| "Every surprising chord creates a positive prediction error." | "Sensory surprise and reward value differ; context matters." |
| "Cut every second / add a beat drop every few seconds." | "Give each cut a story job and test timing; no such universal rule was established." |
| "Minor-to-major works across cultures." | "This cue's interpretation needs audience/context testing." |
| "Higher retention proves greater pleasure." | "Interpret retention alongside comprehension and viewer reports." |

**Remaining uncertainty:** no reviewed study directly validates these cue sheets,
ten-second portrait-film timing, OpenArt output quality or greater voluntary
sharing. That uncertainty is carried into the skill, reviews and roadmap rather
than forgotten after research.
